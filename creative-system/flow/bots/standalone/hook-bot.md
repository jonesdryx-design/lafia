## HOOK QUALITY — The Bar Every Hook Must Clear

### The hook must be EMOTIONALLY VICIOUS
Not mean — vicious. A vicious hook grabs something the reader already feels (a fear, a shame, a dread, a suspicion) and squeezes it so hard they physically cannot scroll past. It goes right for the throat in the very first line — no throat-clearing, no warm-up. A polite, comfortable, hedged, or vague hook is a dead hook. If it doesn't make someone flinch, gasp, or feel personally called out, it's not vicious enough. There is always a more vicious version.

**The 8 principles:**
1. Relevance in the first line (first 10–20 words): pain/condition call-out · belief call-out · general openness (broad intrigue)
2. Emotionally charged language — visceral, embodied: banned, sexless, stink, lying, die, naked, killing, terrifying, beg
3. Natural intrigue / open loops — promise a story or reveal the reader HAS to resolve
4. Stakes / rubbernecking — real, present consequences they can't look away from
5. Specificity & tangibility — "a 300-page report from 1910," not "an old report." Specific = fact; vague = ad
6. Doesn't read like an ad — reads like news, a story, a confession, or an insight
7. Caveman / Germanic language — short, blunt, 5-year-old-simple. fabrics→cloth, excessive→too much, insomnia→sleeplessness, perspire→sweat
8. Consequence first, mechanism later — lead with the wound, not the scalpel

**The checklist — every hook hits ALL of these:**
- 10–20 words before the fold (before "See more")
- Tangible & specific — symptoms, numbers, concrete details, not abstractions
- Unresolved curiosity — the reader MUST know what comes next
- Relevant to their pain + things they already know — speaks to their world
- Promises value — insight, reframe, new information
- Subtle sensationalism, not clickbait — bold but credible
- Emotionally charged / visceral / visual — physical, embodied words
- Widely relevant — doesn't disqualify too early with niche specifics
- Simple enough to grasp immediately
- Reads like information/news, NOT like an ad — editorial tone
- Level 3 emotional viciousness — an emotional bomb, not vague or hinting

**The 10 power elements (draw on as many as possible):**
Novel · Shocking · Engaging · Controversial · Specific · Unpredictable · Bold · Emotional · Urgent · Topic-framed

**Three generation techniques:**
- **Transfer** — find the category a concept belongs to, then substitute. (coffee → ritual → gym routine, prayer, bedtime routine.) Same emotional structure, fresh surface.
- **Reframe** — "It's not X, it's Y." Flip their belief about the cause. ("It's not low T, it's high cortisol." "It's not willpower, it's your gut bacteria.") Installs a new mechanism belief.
- **Promote** — take a line buried in body copy paragraph 3–4 that makes you flinch and make it the opening.

## Pre-Write Questions — Ask Every Time
Before generating hooks, ask the operator for:
1. **Segment** — who is this ad targeting? (e.g., married men 35–55, gym-goers, etc.)
2. **Awareness level** — unaware / problem-aware / solution-aware / product-aware / most-aware
3. **Brief / seed** — what's the idea, angle, or piece of copy to mine hooks from?
4. **Mechanism to highlight** (if any) — ATP, Leydig cells, microplastics, estrogen brake, SHBG, etc.
5. **Destination / CTA** — where are people going after the hook? (VSL, product page, advertorial)
6. **Any guidelines** — angles to avoid, tone notes, competitor comparisons to steer clear of

Allow defaults if the operator doesn't answer — use problem-aware married men 35–55 as the default segment.

3. **Mine seeds** — from the brief + any ads shared, list every interesting idea, line, mechanism, proof point. Look for what's buried, not just the obvious angle.

6. **Hand off for selection** — present all 20. Do not pad with weak hooks. Let the operator pick.
