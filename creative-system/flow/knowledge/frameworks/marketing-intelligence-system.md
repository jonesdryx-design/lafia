# Marketing Intelligence System (MIS)
Source: Creative Diversity Decoded (Notion)

A systematic way to collect, organize, and deploy facts about your audience, market, and
product to create compelling ads. Think of it as your **fact pantry** — raw ingredients you
pull from and wrap in different angles (breaking news, confession, discovery, conspiracy)
to create messages that stop the scroll.

**The 4 components:**
1. **Avatar Facts** — Raw truths about your audience
2. **Market Facts** — Intelligence about the competitive landscape
3. **Product Facts** — The truth about your solution
4. **Psychology Toolkit** — Techniques to reshape understanding

Each fact is a building block. The angle is the wrapper. The combination is the ad.

# PART 1 — AVATAR FACTS

## A. IDENTITY: WHO THEY ARE

**1. Demographics**
The basic factual framework — age, gender, occupation, location, life circumstances.
*Flow default:* Married men 35–55, suburban, household income $80–180K, professional or business owner, teenagers or young kids at home.

**2. Self-Concept Deep Beliefs**
The fundamental "I am someone who..." statements that guide every decision.

**3. Core Problem Statement**
The one sentence they'd use to describe their situation to someone who truly understands.

**4. Cultural Touchstones**
Shared references, events, moments that shaped their worldview.

Objects or images that carry emotional weight beyond literal meaning.
*Flow:* The guest room they almost moved into. The dinner table where conversations used to happen. The weekend plans his wife makes without asking him. His kids calling him "dad" in a tone that sounds like "sir."

"Double your testosterone in 30 days." "Feel like you're 25 again." "The testosterone booster doctors don't want you to know about." "Natural TRT alternative."

**66. How Competitors Frame the Problem**
"Your T is low and that's why you feel bad." (Surface-level — misses the presence/frame angle.) "You need more testosterone." (Product-solution, not mechanism.) "Low T is ruining your performance." (Starts with performance, misses the relationship angle.)
